\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented \ours, an enforcement mechanism at the Tool Broker of tool-using ReAct agents. \ours{} replaces the
string matching of the previous version with three deterministic components: an intent policy with polarity extracted
from the user request, sensitivity labels attached to individual values, and pinning of the values stated by the user.
The three components target exactly the three gaps measured in the previous version. The broker is specified as a
state-transition system, and authorisation safety, confidentiality and integrity are proved under five assumptions, each
of which is measured empirically.

On a new Vietnamese test set, sealed before the defence code existed, \ours{} reduces the number of harmed families from
40 without a defence to 2 of 48. Utility falls by 3.1 points relative to no defence, while tokens and latency per case
increase by at most 1\% relative to the previous version. Against the previous version (6 families), the reduction is
not statistically significant because of a floor effect. The case-level evidence shows that every family the previous
version missed is blocked by exactly the component designed for it. Under two adaptive rounds, \ours{} lets no family
through beyond those that already get through with the original attacks. On AgentDojo, \ours{} keeps the number of
successful attacks low in both English and Vietnamese, at the price of utility on tasks whose destinations come from
tool data.

Future work follows directly from these limitations: a sealed test set large enough for adequate power (about 120
families), formal verification of the propositions against the code, field-level semantics for \cThree{} to catch
changed numbers and units, a user-confirmation channel for destinations taken from tool data, tracking of values through
transformations outside (A4), and multi-turn tasks.
