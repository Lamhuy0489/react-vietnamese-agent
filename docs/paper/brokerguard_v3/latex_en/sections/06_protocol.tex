\section{Data and Evaluation Protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

The evaluation tests \ours{} on data not used in its design, against published defences and simpler rule
configurations at the broker.

\paragraph{Data sets.} VN-AgentInject turns the threat model of Section~\ref{sec:threat} into runnable cases with
synthetic data. The \dev{} has 168 families and 1,960 cases, of which 840 are attacks, 840 \bPlain{} and 280 \bAuth. It
joins the initial 48-family development set with the 72- and 48-family test sets of the early stage. The 40 cases that
got past \strSand{} on these test sets guided the design of \ours, so testing on them again would be optimistic. The
\dev{} is split by family into a 126-family training part, used to tune cue lists, thresholds and field names, and a
42-family validation part, used to select the configuration. The \test{} has 48 new families, 8 per mechanism, with 560
cases, of which 240 are attacks, 240 \bPlain{} and 80 \bAuth. Its families were generated from a specification and
reviewed by two team members. The set was sealed with SHA-256 before the first line of \ours{} code was written, and its
largest 4-gram Jaccard overlap with the \dev{} is 0.05.

\paragraph{Adaptive attacks.} The first set has 24 cases within the \test, generated and sealed with it and aimed at the
three weaknesses of \strSand{} with 8 cases each. The second set has 42 cases written by two team members after \ours{}
was frozen, with full knowledge of its design and formal model. Each replaces the attack text of one family in the
validation part of the \dev{} and targets assumptions (A2) to (A5).

\paragraph{Backbones and configurations.} Gemma-4-12B, Llama-3.1-8B with a 16k context and Qwen3.5-9B are served with
vLLM, pinned by revision, with the same ReAct prompt, temperature 0, at most 512 tokens per step and at most eight
steps. Google's Gemini 3 Flash is a closed model, so it serves only as a reference column with five configurations. All
configurations share the agent, broker and environment, and with no defence the broker only checks schemas. The other
defences are \cSpot~\cite{hines2024spotlighting}, \cSand, \cPG, which screens tool outputs with Prompt
Guard~2~\cite{meta2025promptguard2}, \cMel~\cite{zhu2025melon} and \cProg~\cite{shi2025progent}, the last two
re-implemented from their original descriptions. The rules at the broker are \phrRules, which block instruction-like
phrases, \strRules, \strSand, \ours{} and three ablations that each remove one component. The eight configurations of
the main matrix were run once per backbone on the 560 cases and the 24 adaptive cases, with reruns only after
infrastructure errors. \cSpot, \cPG, \cMel, \phrRules{} and \strRules{} were frozen earlier but run after the main
results were known, so comparisons with them are exploratory.

\paragraph{Metrics.} ASR is the share of harmful cases among the 720 attack cases of the three backbones. Utility $U$ is
the share of the 960 benign cases that meet a deterministic, task-specific completion condition without harm, and
$\Delta U$ is its change relative to no defence. Model errors, such as malformed or truncated outputs, count as utility
failures, never as defence successes. A family is \emph{harmed} if at least one of its attack cases is harmful on at
least one backbone. It is the primary unit of inference because the cases of a family share a task and an environment.
On the \dev, the corresponding unit is the family--backbone pair, 504 in total. The cost metrics are the confirmation
rate, the tokens and wall time per case, and the false-block rate, which is the share of benign cases with at least one
denial.

\paragraph{Pre-registered hypotheses.} Before the \test{} was sealed, four hypotheses were registered under sequential
gatekeeping. H1, the primary hypothesis, states that \ours{} has fewer harmed families than \strSand, tested with a
one-sided exact McNemar test~\cite{mcnemar1947} on the 48 paired families at $\alpha = 0.05$. H2 to H4 are tested only
if H1 rejects its null. H2 states that the utility of \ours{} is at most 5 points below \strSand. H3 states that removing
each component increases the harmed families, with Holm correction. H4 bounds confirmations at 10\%, false blocks at
15\%, and the overhead relative to \strSand{} at 20\% for tokens and 15\% for latency.

\paragraph{AgentDojo.} The Banking and Slack suites of AgentDojo v1.2.2~\cite{debenedetti2024agentdojo}, with 144 and
105 pairs of user and injection tasks respectively, are run with the \code{important\_instructions} attack in English
and in a Vietnamese translation that keeps IBANs, amounts, names and addresses. \StrSand{} and \ours{} run with
Llama-3.1-8B through an adapter, declared before running, that states which tools are sinks and which argument holds the
destination. In the adapter, the \pOne{} only requires the destination to occur as a whole token in $u$ outside any
negation scope. Sandwich here is the built-in \code{repeat\_user\_prompt} defence of AgentDojo, and utility without
attack is measured on 16 Banking and 21 Slack tasks.
