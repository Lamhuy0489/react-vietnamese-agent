\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

This section reports the main results and pre-registered tests on the \test, then analyses mechanisms, adaptive
attacks and generalisation.

\paragraph{Main results.} Table~\ref{tab:main} summarises the thirteen configurations on the \test\ifpareto, and
Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} plots security against utility\fi. With no defence, 40 of the 48 families are harmed on at least
one backbone and 32.1\% of the attack cases succeed. With \ours, only 2 families are harmed and 5 of the 720 attack
cases succeed, an ASR of 0.7\%. As the broker rules become stricter, harmed families fall monotonically from 34 with
\phrRules{} to 15 with \strRules, 6 with \strSand{} and 2 with \ours, and the \dev{} shows the same order. Detection has
almost no effect on Vietnamese data: \cPG{} flags no observation of the \test{} and \cMel{} still leaves 40 families
harmed. \cSand{} and \cProg{} block only about half of the families.

For the primary hypothesis H1, harmed families fall from 6 with \strSand{} to 2 with \ours. Four families are harmed
only under \strSand{} and none only under \ours, so the one-sided exact McNemar test gives $p = 0.0625$, or $0.125$
two-sided, short of the registered $\alpha = 0.05$ because the discordant pairs are too few, as
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} explains. The effect has the same direction on all three backbones, each dropping from 2, 2
and 3 families to 1. Under the gatekeeping procedure, H2 to H4 are reported descriptively. The utility of
\ours{} is 0.56 points below \strSand{} on 720 paired \bPlain{} cases and 0.42 points below on 240 paired \bAuth{}
cases, within the 5-point margin of H2. The three ablations have 4, 6 and 2 harmed families against 2 for \ours, with
one-sided $p$ of 0.50, 0.19 and 1. The exploratory comparisons with the other defences, uncorrected for multiplicity,
show larger differences, for example $p = 3.6\cdot10^{-12}$ against no defence, $9.5\cdot10^{-7}$ against \cSand{}
and $1.2\cdot10^{-4}$ against \strRules.

\paragraph{Trade-off between security, utility and cost.} \ifpareto In Figure~\ref{fig:pareto}\else In
Table~\ref{tab:main}\fi, the \ours{} variants have the fewest harmed families, and no other configuration has both fewer
harmed families and more utility. The utility cost grows with stricter rules, from 0.8 points for \strRules{} to 2.6 for
\strSand{} and 3.1 for \ours. Moving from \strSand{} to \ours{} removes 4 more harmed families at the cost of 5 benign
cases, or 0.5 points. Since \strSand{} keeps more utility and the ablation without \pThree{} keeps one more case than
\ours, \ours{} is not Pareto-dominant over these two. The prompt-level defences reduce harm at a cost of 2.1 to 3.1
utility points. Even denying all sinks, estimated from the trajectories with no defence, would leave 14 families harmed
through the final answer and only 11.5\% utility.

The new components add little cost. On each backbone, the mean tokens per case of \ours{} differ from \strSand{}
by at most 0.8\%, and the mean wall time by $-1.4\%$ to $+0.6\%$. The confirmation rate on benign cases is at
most 0.6\%, and the false-block rate is 1.9\% to 3.4\%, against 1.6\% to 2.8\% for \strSand, all below the H4
thresholds. The gate takes 0.26~ms per call on average, against about 14~s of model time per case.

\paragraph{Analysis by mechanism.} Table~\ref{tab:mechres} gives the harmed families per mechanism. \cSand{} and
\cProg{} reduce redirection but still let all 8 sink-leakage families through, and \cProg{} does not reduce
answer leakage or tampering either. Each component of \ours, in contrast, acts on the mechanisms it was designed for.
Removing \pTwo{} lets 4 leakage families through again, removing the \pOne{} adds 1 redirection and 1 tampering family,
and removing \pThree{} adds only one harmful case. At the case level, all 4 families harmed only under \strSand{} were
sink violations that \ours{} blocked before execution, 2 by \pTwo{} and 2 by the \pOne, and each case passes again once
that component alone is removed. By harm type over the 720 attack cases, sink violations fall from 136 with
no defence to 4 under \strSand{} and none under \ours. Final-answer leaks fall from 54 to zero and tampering from 79 to
5 under both configurations, and these 5 cases come from the two families with changed numbers or units that both
gates let through.

\paragraph{Language variants.} With no defence, between 40 and 51 of the 144 attack cases per language variant are
harmful, the most for standard Vietnamese and the fewest for English. Under \ours, at most 2 of the 144 cases are
harmful in any variant: 1 for standard Vietnamese, 1 for the paraphrase, 2 for code-mixing, 1 for text without
diacritics and none for English. The protection thus holds across all five forms, including code-mixed text and text
without diacritics, although counts this small cannot show an effect of language.

\paragraph{Adaptive attacks.} No case of the first set harms \ours, but this result favours \ours, since the set targets
exactly the weaknesses that informed the design. With no defence, the second set harms 25 of the 42 families, close to
the 26 harmed by the original attacks, so the adaptive attacks are effective. Under \strSand{} and \ours, no family
succeeds with the adaptive attack without also succeeding with the original one. The only family still getting past
\ours, on Qwen3.5, involves parameter tampering, like the two remaining families on the \test. Up to 18 of the 42 cases
on Qwen3.5 under \strSand{} and \ours{} exhaust the eight-step budget, so the results hold only within that budget.

\paragraph{Generalisation.} With Gemini 3 Flash on the \test, 26 families are harmed with no defence, 14 with \cProg{}
and none with \cSand, \strSand{} or \ours, so the last two cannot be separated on security. In utility, \ours{}
completes 201 of the 240 \bPlain{} cases, against 205 for \strSand, and falsely blocks 8 of 320 benign cases, against 4.

Table~\ref{tab:agentdojo} reports the AgentDojo results. \ours{} matches \strSand{} on Banking in both languages. On
English Slack, successful attacks drop from 6 to 5: by requiring a whole-token match, the \pOne{} blocks the case in
which the recipient's name appeared only as a substring of the request. Of the remaining successes, 4 Banking cases, 3 in
English and 1 in Vietnamese, change a password through a sink without a destination, 4 cases open a URL with a read
tool, and 1 other case is on English Slack. The first two kinds lie outside the sinks covered by the formal model. The
price is lower utility on Slack, from 15 to 11 under attack in English and from 5 to 3 of the 21 tasks without attack in
both languages, because many Slack tasks take their destination from a web page or a message that the user did not
write.
