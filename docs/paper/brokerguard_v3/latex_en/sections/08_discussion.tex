\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

This section explains why the primary test fell short of significance, then discusses each component, utility and the
limits of the evaluation.

\paragraph{Statistical power of the primary test.} The power analysis for H1 assumed that \strSand{} would let through
about 14 of the 48 families, as on the 48-family set of the early stage, which gave an estimated power of 91\% if
\ours{} blocked 8 more. On the \test{} it let through only 6, mainly because harmed family--backbone pairs in sink
leakage fell from 9 to 1. With such a low baseline, few discordant pairs can be observed, so the power of the test is
only about 26\%, and about 120 families would be needed for 80\% power. The result therefore reflects a floor effect,
not evidence that the two configurations are equivalent. Other data point the same way. On the \dev, harmed
family--backbone pairs fall from 36 to 4, and rescoring the 40 cases that got past \strSand{} in the early stage gives
38 harmful cases under \strSand{} and 2 under \ours. These data were used during development, so they are diagnostic
only, and no run was added after the results were known to search for significance.

\paragraph{Contribution of each component.} Replaying the reference trajectories of the validation part without a model
isolates the gate, although these data informed the design. Of the 210 attack trajectories, 25 are harmful under
\strSand{} and 2 under \ours, and removing the \pOne, \pTwo{} or \pThree{} raises this to 7, 6 and 13. Scored alone on
the sink calls of these trajectories, the \pOne{} allows all 155 plain and 65 authorised benign calls, as the \rDest{}
does, and blocks 105 of the 190 attack calls, against 85, and all 35 unrequested side-effect calls, against 15. The
models rarely propose such calls themselves, however, so on the \test{} the difference shows in only 2 families.
\PThree{} has no measurable effect on the \test{} yet. The two families that still get through admit two explanations,
not yet checked case by case. Either the changed value was not among the pinned literals, violating the complete-pins
assumption, or the literal was still present next to another value, a case that Proposition~\ref{prop:int} does not
exclude. Both call for field-level semantics in \pThree{} rather than a presence check on literals.

\paragraph{Utility and language.} On both benign branches, \ours{} loses at most 0.6 points relative to \strSand, but
both configurations lie about 3 points below no defence, partly through false blocks in 24 against 19 of the 960 benign
cases. On the \dev, false blocks come mostly from the base rules, and for Llama-3.1 from \pThree, because this model
tends to rewrite values stated by the user. Absolute utility is low for every configuration, 42.8\% even with no
defence, owing to the limits of small models such as exhausted steps or truncated outputs. In deployment, \emph{needs
confirmation} cases could be passed to the user, recovering utility without granting authority to untrusted content. The
\pOne{} depends on the language because polarity is derived from lists of negation, exclusion and contrast cues, and a
negation outside these lists could let the gate allow a forbidden destination.

\paragraph{Validity and limitations.} The data are synthetic, the tools are mocks and each task has a single turn. The
\test{} was generated from a specification by code and reviewed by the team that developed the defence. Although it was
sealed before any \ours{} code existed, its authors may have unintentionally chosen cases that suit the design. The
second adaptive set was written by team members in a single round on the \dev. The open models have only 8 to 12 billion
parameters, and AgentDojo was run with one model. The propositions are proved on the specification, not verified
formally against the code. Unauthorised reads are out of scope, and under \ours{} 18 of the 720 attack cases still
contain an unauthorised read call, against 20 under \strSand. \PTwo{} does not track values through transformations
outside the transformation class of Section~\ref{sec:formal}, but the transformed-leak scorer found no such case, so
this assumption remains untested.
